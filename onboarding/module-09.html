The developing athlete

This is where most arm damage starts. Growth, not velocity, is the risk to manage.

The vulnerable window Bult et al., 2018 · Cumming et al., 2017 · via Serio, Baseball America, Feb 2026
Watch
  • Growth faster than about 3 in a year: 74% higher injury risk, in any sport.
  • The 6 months after a growth spurt are the most vulnerable.
  • "Adolescent awkwardness": command and coordination can drop because the body changed, not the mechanics.
Strength
  • Energy Interference: build it with no ceiling.
  • Build physical preparation before velocity demands, not after an injury forces it.
Throwing
  • Match demands to biological readiness, not the recruiting calendar.
  • A younger arm may need a lower rolling Day Score baseline.
Nutrition
  • Start the training first and let hunger rise on its own.
  • Point families to the free VeloU nutrition course.
Why it matters

55% of pros who tore a UCL had elbow problems as kids. Surgery before about 19.5 cut first-rounders' odds of reaching MLB nearly in half. Protecting this window is the mission (Module 1).

Throwing
  • Velocity-gated. Radar gun at every session.
  • Intent read off his own RPE Conversion Chart. The gate is the safety mechanism.
  • Lower his rolling Day Score baseline.
Strength
  • Regress without hesitation: P1, even if he trained at P3 before.
  • Force Control P1 and Acceleration P1 are built as re-entry points.
Coordinate
  • The plan belongs to the medical and rehab staff and the referring surgeon.
  • Never substitute a standard phase for an RTT plan.
The first year back
  • After Tommy John, MLB pitchers return with the same velocity and spin.
  • Fastball usage and performance dip in year one and normalize in year two. Trust in the arm takes longer than the tissue.
Communication

Returning athletes and their families need more updates, not fewer. Every velocity gate cleared is progress they should hear about. Update the athlete and family every four weeks. Check in with medical staff every eight weeks, or sooner if any hiccups arise.

The professional

Mature tissue, adapted range of motion, and a career on the line. Read his numbers against pro norms.

Velocity matters less to the elbow as athletes mature R² of velocity vs. elbow varus torque · Manzi et al., 2023
Reading his numbers
  • Energy Interference baseline is 80+. ER/IR range-of-motion adaptations change how end-range testing reads.
  • A low kneeling throw in a pro points to hip clearance.
  • No pro lives in a reliant energy profile.
Why
  • Pros use trunk and pelvis rotation to offload the elbow.
  • In VeloU's data, ages 23-25 still make up about 31% of Tommy John cases. Mature does not mean immune.
Programming
  • Advanced athletes may live in P2 to P3 for good.
  • Balance stress: when stress from throwing rises, stress from lifting comes down (Module 5).
Working with his club
  • For a healthy professional, the point person is our Director of Player Development, Coach Tom Jankins.
  • For an injured athlete, the point person is Dr. Nicholas Serio.

Teams

A team is a college or club organization we work with. Training is strictly remote, so the standard travels and the room does not.

Who counts as a team
  • A college or club organization.
  • Trained remotely only. No onsite visits, other than potentially the initial evaluation.
Evaluation
  • Every athlete is put through an evaluation, in a team rotating format.
  • If the team comes onsite, it includes the force plate mound and our other tech.
Zones
  • Zones do not apply. Team athletes are trained remotely, so there is no VeloU floor to zone (Module 8).
Point people
  • Sam Byrns, General Manager, and Ben Adams, Director of Remote Training, are the two point people for every team.
  • The team's coaches hear from them, not from an individual coach.
