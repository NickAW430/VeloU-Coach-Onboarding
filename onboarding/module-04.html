4.4

The phase engine

The phase sets load and how the rep is executed. It has nothing to do with throwing; that's the split (Module 5).

P1Load band opens, constraint loosest
P2Load climbs, constraint sharpens
P3Load peaks, constraint tightest, closest to power
LinearP1 → P2 → P3

A full build, bottom to top.

Non-linearEnter where diagnosed

Hold, advance, or regress at each re-diagnosis. An advanced athlete may live in P2 to P3 for good.

Rule

The phase describes current capacity, not a ladder. A returning or beat-up athlete drops to P1, even if he trained at P3 before. Regressing is normal, not a failure.

4.5

The prep: 15 minutes, every day

Every strength day opens with prep. It covers what the lifting alone doesn't.

1Expressive

Level-based dose of crawl, sprint, jump, carry, rotate. Sprint and jump at least 3 days a week.

2Vestibular

Rolling, inversion, hanging, turning, cartwheels, unstable carries. Challenge him off a stable head.

3Midline

Hollow, rotational, and compression work on upper days. A tall spine while the limbs move.

4.6

Durations: runway, not load

Same exercises and day layout in every length. Longer blocks buy accumulation, never a heavier load.

2 weeks

W1W2

Peak fortnight. Re-diagnose at W2. No separate deload.

4 weeks · default

W1W2W3W4 ▽

Build, build, peak. W4 deloads and re-diagnoses.

8 weeks

W1W2W3W4 ▽W5W6W7W8 ▽

Two 4-week ticks. The striped second tick adds volume, not load.

Bar heights show the shape of the wave, not exact values. Movement Literacy P1 and P2 also offer a 12-week wave.

4.8

When decisions change

Phase, split, and duration change only at a re-diagnosis point: the end of the block. Never mid-block.

Advance

He raised the metric that put him there, across the full load band, not on one good day.

Hold

Progress is real but incomplete. Run the phase again.

Regress

After a layoff, an injury, or a block where the held stop broke down. Without hesitation.
